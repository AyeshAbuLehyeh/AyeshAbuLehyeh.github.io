Ayesh Abu Lehyeh

Ph.D. Candidate in Computer Science, University of Vermont

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at the University of Vermont, advised by Prof. Safwan Wshah. My research is in computer vision, focused on visual geo-localization: finding where an image was taken by matching it to satellite imagery, from large-scale cross-view retrieval to real-time, fine-grained pose estimation. I also work on localization from RF signals with calibrated uncertainty, and on vision-language reasoning for spatial understanding.

Before my Ph.D., I spent four years at Orange Jordan as an RF engineer and senior data analyst. In summer 2026 I interned at NextNav, building probabilistic machine learning models for 5G positioning. I hold an M.S. in Computer Science from the University of Vermont and a B.S. in Electrical Engineering from the University of Jordan.

I am on the job market for research scientist and applied scientist roles starting fall 2027.

Email/ CV/ Google Scholar/ GitHub/ LinkedIn

Research Interests

My work is aimed at spatial intelligence: giving machines a reliable sense of where they are and how the scene around them is laid out. Within that, I am interested in cross-view image geo-localization, 3D vision (reconstruction, feature matching, pose estimation), generative models (flow matching, diffusion), vision-language models, and multimodal localization.
